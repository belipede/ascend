## Juror: Competitor Analyst

**Recommendation:** CONDITIONAL. The condition is to build for Ben's own use only, and not to claim a market differentiator until it has been tested against Strides and TickTick.

**Strongest argument:**
- Goal-linked progress is the one real gap I found. Most habit trackers count streaks without showing whether the habit moves the goal it serves (OPINION; one source argues this, [Griply roundup](https://griply.app/compare/best-habit-tracker-apps), but Griply is a competitor that ranks itself first).
- Strides is the closest rival and already markets "Habit Tracker + Goals". It has about 4.7–4.8 App Store ratings, a free tier limited to 3 trackers, and Plus from $4.99/month (FACT, per [App Store listing aggregator](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/strides-habit-tracker-goals/672401817?country=au) and [crm.org review](https://crm.org/news/strides-app-review)). So the goal → habit link is not unique.
- What I did not find is a polished app that covers the whole chain: goal, milestone, habit, task, and one progress number with no double counting. Absence from a few searches is not proof of absence (OPINION).

**Main risk:**
- The market is crowded and users are used to cheap or one-time pricing. Streaks is a one-time purchase of about $5–6, Things 3 is a one-time purchase (about $10, unconfirmed for 2026), and TickTick Premium is about $36/year and bundles habits, tasks and a calendar (FACT, but sources conflict; see [Habitdoom comparison](https://habitdoom.com/blog/best-habit-tracker-iphone-2026), [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/ticktick-vs-things-3/)).
- Todoist Pro is about $60/year with AI and 80+ integrations (FACT, one source).
- Commercial headroom looks thin. A paywall on a solo-built app would face free Apple Reminders and these incumbents (OPINION).
- Strides is reported as cluttered and "too complicated", with goal-versus-habit confusion (WEAK evidence, from [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/streaks-habitica-way-of-life-strides-habit-tracking-apps-ranked-2026), a blog with no stated methodology). Ascend's five-level hierarchy could repeat that complexity.

**Key assumption:** Users want goals tied to daily actions and will tolerate the setup effort. I have no evidence for this beyond competitor marketing pages.

**Suggested improvement:**
- Before Phase 6, run a cheap check. Read the 1–3 star App Store reviews for Strides, TickTick habits and Streaks, and look for complaints about goal linkage.
- Keep the MVP's single-source progress and no double counting as the headline feature. The Today screen and widget are table stakes and won't set the app apart.
- Consider cutting the 5-level hierarchy to goal → habit/task, with milestones optional.
- Treat these as standard expectations (OPINION): interactive widgets, reminders, streaks, recurrence, and export.
- Add Apple Shortcuts/App Intents early. That is a cheap integration and fits the stack.
- The "no guilt" tone is differentiating only if the product follows through. Streaks, Way of Life and Habitica mostly use streak pressure (OPINION).

**Confidence:** LOW

**Evidence quality:** WEAK. It rests on listicles and competitor blogs whose prices disagree, plus App Store aggregator pages. I did not read primary review text or the official pricing pages. Third-party pricing numbers should be verified before use.

**Answers to my questions:**
- **What already exists?** Streaks, Way of Life, Habitify, Done and Productive (habits). Things 3, Todoist and TickTick (tasks, with TickTick adding habits). Strides and Griply (habits plus goals). Apple Reminders is the free baseline.
- **What do competitors do well?** Fast capture, widgets, simple streaks, and cross-platform sync (TickTick, Todoist).
- **Where do users experience friction?** Free-tier caps (Way of Life, Strides), dated or cluttered UI, and goal-versus-habit confusion. All of these are WEAK evidence.
- **Which features are standard expectations?** Widgets, reminders, recurrence, streaks, and iCloud or cross-device sync. Sync is a gap for a local-only app.
- **What could we do noticeably better?** One clear progress number from goal down to task, a low-guilt tone, and a simple setup flow.
