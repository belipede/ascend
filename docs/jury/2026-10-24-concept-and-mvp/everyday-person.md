## Juror: Everyday Person
Recommendation: CONDITIONAL

Strongest argument: The pitch, "see why today's actions matter, without guilt or a complicated system", is what I'd want. Reminders gives me a list but never tells me whether I'm getting anywhere. The "Read 24 books → read 10 pages today" chain is easy to understand in one sentence (OPINION). The MVP cuts are sensible for me: no review notifications, no calendar views, no statistics screen, no encouragement engine (FACT: PRODUCT-BRIEF.md, Deferred list).

Main risk: Setup work. To get any value I have to create a goal, a milestone, a habit and a task, and link them. On a tired Tuesday that's more effort than typing "read book" into Reminders (OPINION). The second risk is the bad week. The brief lists streaks and "overdue carries forward", but it doesn't say what happens when I miss days. Streaks that reset to zero make me feel guilty, and a pile of overdue items makes me stop opening the app (OPINION). The brief says "without guilt", but I found no design for the missed-day experience (FACT: absent from the brief and roadmap).

Key assumption: People will do the up-front linking work because they'll value the connection afterwards. Nothing in the brief tests this (FACT: "No users, no research"). I'd mostly use Today, and the goal layer would be the part I skip.

Suggested improvement:
- Make the linking optional. Habits and tasks should work alone, and a goal can be attached later. Then the app is at least as easy as Reminders, with the goal view as a bonus.
- Allow creating a habit or task from inside a goal in one step, with a starter template such as "Read 24 books" that pre-fills the pieces.
- Design the missed-day experience before building streaks. Show "5 of the last 7 days" rather than a broken streak. Make "rest/pause" one tap. Let overdue tasks be rescheduled in bulk or dropped, not just carried forward.
- Keep reminders to a few defaults. Per-item reminders mean one more thing to set up for every item, and many reminders add up to notification fatigue.
- Keep the widget check-off. It's the best time-saver in the plan, because I wouldn't have to open the app.
- Run the 1–2 week personal trial (Phase 5) as the real test. Before Phase 6, ask 3–5 ordinary people to set up one goal and say what they did on day 3 and day 10. That costs almost nothing.

Confidence: LOW

Evidence quality: WEAK. It rests only on the brief and roadmap, plus my general sense of how ordinary people behave. I haven't seen a prototype, and this is a simulated perspective, not user research.

Answers to my questions:
- Understand without instructions? Probably yes for Today, habits and tasks. I'm unsure about how goals, milestones, plans and habits relate. "Plan" and "milestone" may be confusing (OPINION). This needs checking with real people.
- Easier than Apple Reminders? Not today. It's only easier if quick-add is as fast as Reminders and the linking is optional.
- Save time or create more work? It creates more work at setup and saves a little afterwards, through the widget and progress that updates itself. The net result is unproven.
- Keep using it after a difficult week? Only if returning feels painless. That depends on the missed-day design, which is currently undefined. This needs checking with real people.
- Encourage without guilt? The intent is right, but the brief gives no concrete mechanism. Streak counters are the main guilt risk, and the encouragement engine is deferred.
