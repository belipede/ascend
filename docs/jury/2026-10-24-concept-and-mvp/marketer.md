## Juror: Marketer

**Recommendation:** CONDITIONAL. I support building the personal MVP. I oppose any public-launch or commercial spend until the conditions below are met.

**Strongest argument:** The "why today matters" pitch is easy to say in one sentence, and the "Read 24 books" example makes it easy to picture. That is a good base for first-minute understanding. The MVP is also free of cloud, subscription and AI features, so a privacy-first, local-only, no-account, no-guilt positioning is available at no extra cost. (OPINION, based on the brief.)

**Main risk:** There is no defined audience, so there is no positioning. The brief names no primary user (its open question 3) and has no research and no users (FACT: PRODUCT-BRIEF.md, "Situation"). The goal → habit → task chain is probably not unique. I believe the habit-tracker and to-do categories are crowded and that some existing apps already link goals to habits. That is unverified, and I did no competitor research. Competitor-analyst should cover it. If the loop is not clearly different, Ascend competes on taste alone. Taste alone is a weak reason to switch, because users would have to move their data and routines.

**Key assumption:** People who have goals will take the extra step of defining a plan and milestones, and will keep doing so after the first week. That is the main friction point. A blank "create a goal" screen is where onboarding usually loses people. (ESTIMATE/OPINION: no data is available.)

**Suggested improvement:**
1. Before Phase 6, write a one-sentence audience hypothesis. One example is "people with 1–3 big yearly goals who find to-do apps too shallow and habit apps too disconnected". Then test it with 5–10 interviews, or a no-code landing page or TestFlight, at close to zero cost.
2. Put the first-minute experience in the MVP. This means a starter template such as "Read 24 books" that creates the whole chain in under 60 seconds, and a Today screen that shows the goal each item serves. Without it the differentiator stays hidden. It costs little and should be in Phase 2.
3. During Ben's 1–2 weeks of personal use, record one thing: did the goal link change what he did that day? That is the earliest evidence on the differentiator.
4. For the first 100 users, if the idea passes validation, try these channels in order. Use only what Ben approves, since he approves any publishing:
   - Ben's own network
   - TestFlight invites
   - Reddit and productivity communities (read each community's self-promotion rules first)
   - A short build-in-public log
   - An App Store listing built around the "no account, data stays on your device" claim
5. Keep claims honest. Do not promise outcomes such as "achieve your goals". Do not use guilt-based streak or notification language.

**Confidence:** MEDIUM for the process advice, LOW for market viability.

**Evidence quality:** WEAK. This rests on the brief and roadmap only, plus general knowledge of the category. There is no user data and I ran no searches.

**Answers to my questions:**
- **Who is it for?** It is undefined. The best hypothesis is self-directed adults with a few long-term goals who are frustrated with disconnected tools. This is an OPINION and needs testing.
- **Value in the first minute?** Not yet. The pitch is clear, but the MVP has no onboarding or template, so the value would have to be worked out by the user.
- **First 100 users?** Warm network, then TestFlight, then community posts, and only after personal use shows a real effect. There is nothing to promote before that.
- **Why choose it over alternatives?** The connected loop, calm tone and local-only privacy could be reasons to choose it. None of them is proven, and competitors may match them.
